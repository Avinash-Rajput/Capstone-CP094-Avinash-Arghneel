Social media has a powerful influence on how young people perform at school. Every day, students scroll past carefully edited photos of classmates who seem happier, richer and more successful than they are. This constant comparison slowly wears down self-esteem, and a student who feels worthless rarely has the energy to study.

Stress is another serious issue that I have noticed among my friends. A worried mind struggles to remember formulas, and exam nerves become much worse when a student already doubts their own ability. Online bullying adds another layer of stress, because hurtful comments can follow a victim home and appear on their screen at any hour. Some victims stop attending classes altogether, which leaves gaps in their knowledge that are hard to fill later.

However, there is a positive side too. Students who feel isolated sometimes find online communities where they can share worries about exams and receive encouragement from strangers. Many of these groups are run by older students who remember how hard their own exams felt.

To sum up, social media can harm or help depending on how it is used. Counsellors, teachers and parents need to talk openly about comparison, anxiety and cyberbullying, so that students can protect both their confidence and their academic results.
