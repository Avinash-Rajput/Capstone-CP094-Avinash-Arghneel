Grades suffer whenever students hand their attention over to platforms that were built to hold it. Notifications buzz on a phone every few minutes, and with each buzz, homework, revision and reading lose focus. Procrastination begins as one harmless check, yet platforms profit from scrolling without end, and an entire hour disappears. Platforms compete for each resource that decides grades, from sleep to focus to attention.

Attention is where the harm begins. Studies of multitasking indicate that, after any interruption, regaining focus takes the mind several minutes. Deep focus is never reached by students whose notifications keep arriving, so grades show hurried and shallow assignments. Essays seem dull next to platforms that always provide entertainment, which feeds procrastination. Grades fall when attention is wasted, and attention is wasted whenever notifications shatter focus.

Next comes sleep. Instead of sleep, plenty of teenagers answer notifications at midnight and scroll through platforms for hours. Morning lectures then find their attention gone, exams find their focus fading, and grades slide lower. Exhausted, they fall into procrastination once more, since difficult tasks seem impossible to start. Procrastination increases as focus weakens, and focus weakens as sleep shrinks.

Banning platforms is not the answer, but controlling them is. Focus and attention return when notifications are switched off, study hours are kept free of phones, and sleep is protected. Within one term, grades usually recover for students who handle procrastination like this. Guarding grades really means guarding attention against platforms.
